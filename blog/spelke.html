Discovering and Using Spelke Segments

Rahul Venkatesh, Klemen Kotar, Lilian Naing Chen, Seungwoo Kim, Luca Thomas Wheeler, Jared Watrous, Ashley Xu, Gia Ancone, Wanhee Lee, Honglin Chen, Daniel Bear, Stefan Stojanov, Daniel Yamins.

Semantic categories are not the only way to slice a scene. Developmental psychology suggests a more useful primitive for manipulation and planning — the Spelke object: a grouping of physical things that reliably move together when acted on by physical forces.

Two contributions

  • SpelkeBench. A dataset of natural images with well-defined Spelke segments, independent of semantic class labels.
  • SpelkeNet. A visual world model that predicts motion distributions. It finds segments through statistical counterfactual probing — virtual pokes whose induced displacements identify what moves together.

Results

SpelkeNet beats supervised alternatives like SegmentAnything on SpelkeBench and improves downstream physical manipulation tasks — the kinds of tasks where knowing what moves together actually matters.
