World Modeling with Probabilistic Structure Integration

Klemen Kotar, Wanhee Lee, Rahul Venkatesh, Honglin Chen, Daniel Bear, Jared Watrous, Simon Kim, Khai Loong Aw, Lilian Naing Chen, Stefan Stojanov, Kevin Feigelis, Imran Thobani, Alex Durango, Khaled Jedoui, Atlas Kazemian, Dan Yamins.

We present PSI, a system for building controllable and promptable world models directly from data. PSI is trained on 1.4 trillion tokens of internet video and organizes its learning as a three-step iterative cycle.

The cycle

  1. Probabilistic prediction. A single model expresses conditional distributions between arbitrary subsets of tokens, much like a language model but extended to vision.
  2. Structure extraction. Zero-shot causal probes pull out low-dimensional properties of the world — optical flow, depth, object segments, controllable pokes.
  3. Integration. Those extracted structures are re-encoded as new token types and folded back into the next training round.

Why it matters

Each cycle compounds. The same network that predicts the next frame also produces state-of-the-art optical flow, self-supervised depth, and object segmentation, and it stays promptable — you can condition on any subset of variables and sample any other subset.
